# Build Brief: sethbrasile.com — Personal Portfolio + CV

**Read this first:** This document gives you the *goals, requirements, supporting
facts, and copy direction* for a new website. It deliberately says **nothing about
visual design, layout, color, or components** — those decisions are 100% yours. Use
your own judgment to make it beautiful, fast, and distinctive.

Two more rules about the writing:
1. **Write the marketing copy yourself.** Below you'll find angles, value props, and
   raw facts — not finished sentences. Do **not** paste my phrasings verbatim. Turn
   the points into your own polished copy, following the Voice Rules in §4.
2. **Everything factual must come from §6 (The Data).** Do not invent achievements,
   metrics, employers, or credentials. If a fact isn't in §6, leave it out.

## 1. What we're building & why

A personal brand site for **Seth Brasile** at **sethbrasile.com**, replacing an old,
aspirational single-page site. It is a professional portfolio that showcases his
projects, work history, and skills, and **hosts his CV** (the CV currently lives at

**Primary goal — drive qualified leads to two destinations, in this priority order:**

1. **Primary CTA → his marketing agency, PPMC (Prickly Pear Marketing Co)** at
   **https://pricklypearmarketing.co**, for **SEO and marketing** work.
2. **Secondary CTA → hire Seth directly** for **custom app development and custom
   business / automation development.** This is Seth personally — present it as
   "work with Seth," a direct contact path. (Do **not** brand this as a separate

**Secondary goal — credibility & recruiting.** Seth is also open to the right
fractional / contract / advisory engagement or full-time role, so the CV must also
serve recruiters and hiring managers (see §5 CV page requirements).

**What success looks like:** a visitor quickly understands who Seth is and the depth
behind him, then either (a) heads to PPMC for marketing/SEO, (b) contacts Seth for
build/automation work, or (c) reads/downloads the CV. The site should also own the
"Seth Brasile" search result and read well to AI assistants (see §7–8).

## 2. Audiences & their journeys

Design the content so each of these lands fast:

- **Small/medium business owner needing marketing or SEO** → must be guided to PPMC.
  Speaks plain business English, not jargon. Cares about results and trust.
- **Founder / business needing a custom app, SaaS, or automation built** → must be
  guided to contact Seth directly. More technical, evaluating competence and judgment.
- **Recruiter / hiring manager / fractional-CTO seeker** → wants the CV: depth,
  scope, scale, downloadable resume.
- **Peers / technical readers** (secondary) → projects, writing, homelab; reinforces
  authority and links out to his blog/YouTube.

Make the two commercial CTAs unmistakable and persistent. The CV path can be one
click from the home page but doesn't need to dominate the hero.

## 4. Voice & copy rules

Write in Seth's voice: **confident, concrete, technically literate, zero fluff.**
Mix short impact lines with longer detail lines.

**Do:**
- Concrete specificity over vague claims — name actual tools, scale, dollar amounts,
  team sizes ("led a team of 6," "75+ direct reports," "2,500–3,200 endpoints").
- Plain, strong verbs: built, shipped, designed, led, architected, secured.
- Honest scope: say "proof-of-concept" when it was a POC, "production" when it shipped.
- Lead with the outcome where one exists.

**Don't (banned):**
- No fake enthusiasm: no "passionate about," "rockstar," "synergy," "leverage,"
  "spearhead," "results-driven," "detail-oriented," "self-starter," "team player."
- No "responsible for" / "helped" — use the active verb for what he actually did.
- No "various"/"multiple" without a number.
- No buzzword-salad headers. If a line sounds like a LinkedIn cliché, rewrite it.

Tone calibration: technically dense and credible for the dev/recruiter audience, but
the marketing/PPMC-facing copy must stay in **plain business English** a non-technical
SMB owner reads comfortably.
